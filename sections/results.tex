\section{Results}
\label{sec:results}

To evaluate the effectiveness of our approach in retaining relevant implementations while limiting the effort required to inspect candidate methods, we use two metrics.

The first metric, \textbf{Success Rate}, measures the proportion of business rules for which the reference method is retained among the candidate methods produced by our approach. It shows how often the approach successfully retains the expected implementation of a rule. A higher Success Rate means that fewer business rules are missed during the localization process.

The second metric, \textbf{Recovery Effort Index (REI)}, is inspired by the metric introduced by Antoniol et al.~\cite{Anto02a} to estimate the effort required to recover traceability links between source code and documentation. In their work, REI is defined as the ratio of retrieved documents to the total number of available documents. Following the same principle, we adapt this metric to our context by calculating the proportion of methods retained as candidates relative to the initial set of methods considered. It is calculated as follows:

\[
\textbf{REI} = \frac{\text{Number of retained methods}}{\text{Total number of initial methods}} \times 100
\]
\\
A lower REI indicates that fewer methods need to be examined, reducing the effort required to locate a business rule's implementation. It reflects how the approach narrows the search space while keeping the candidate set manageable for developers.

Both metrics are computed on the final results, providing complementary insights into the effectiveness of the approach.

\subsection{Success Rate}

To evaluate our approach, we applied it to a dataset comprising \textbf{100} textual descriptions of business rules. The ground-truth implementation method was successfully retained for \textbf{94 of the 100 business rules (94\%)}. This result shows that the approach preserves the relevant implementation method for the vast majority of the evaluated rules while substantially narrowing the search space.

\subsection{Recovery Effort Index}

We further analyzed the \textbf{94 cases} in which the reference method was successfully retained after applying the approach. We evaluated the Recovery Effort Index (REI), which measures the proportion of candidate methods relative to the initial set of \textbf{14,578 business-logic methods}. Table~\ref{tab:rei_results} summarizes the results.

\begin{table}[htbp]
\centering
\caption{Candidate-set size and Recovery Effort Index}
\label{tab:rei_results}
\begin{tabular}{lc}
\hline
\textbf{Metric} & \textbf{Value} \\
\hline
Successfully retained cases & 94 \\
Initial business-logic methods & 14,578 \\
Minimum candidate methods & 2 \\
Median candidate methods & 18.5 \\
Maximum candidate methods & 179 \\
Maximum REI & 1.23\% \\
\hline
\end{tabular}
\end{table}

Across the \textbf{94 cases}, the number of candidate methods ranged from \textbf{2} to \textbf{179}, with a median of \textbf{18.5}. The REI remained below \textbf{1.23\%} in all cases, indicating that only a small fraction of the initial business-logic methods was retained for each rule. This reduction in candidate-set size limits the scope of the investigation to methods that are potentially related to the rule description, rather than requiring consideration of the entire business-logic subset. Moreover, the reference method was retained in all \textbf{94 cases} considered in this analysis, showing that the smaller candidate sets were obtained without excluding the expected implementation in these cases. Overall, these results demonstrate the ability of the approach to narrow down potential implementations, thereby reducing the effort required to investigate candidate methods while preserving the reference method.
