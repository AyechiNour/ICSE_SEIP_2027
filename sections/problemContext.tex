\section{Industrial Context and Problem Setting}
\label{sec:industrialContext}

Locating the implementation of business rules in large-scale software systems is a common industrial challenge. Business rules are often embedded in source code without explicit links to their textual descriptions, making it difficult for developers to identify the code that needs to be modified when a rule changes. This challenge is particularly relevant in long-lived systems, where business rules evolve over time and their implementations may be distributed across different parts of the codebase. Failing to identify the appropriate implementation can lead to incomplete or incorrect modifications.

We investigate this problem through an industrial case provided by Berger-Levrault, a software company developing information systems for public administration, healthcare, and education. The system is a large-scale financial management application. It is a long-standing object-oriented application developed in Java that has evolved continuously through maintenance and the addition of new functionalities. The application follows a three-tier architecture and contains \textbf{16,555 classes} and \textbf{115,829 methods}.

The codebase contains different types of classes serving different purposes. Its business vocabulary is relatively consistent, with class names often reflecting concepts from the financial-management domain. However, class names alone are not sufficient to determine where a particular business rule is implemented.

The system is accompanied by documentation describing the business rules implemented in the application. However, the documentation generally does not provide explicit links between a business rule and the methods that implement it. The business rules are organized into a collection of text files, each with a title and divided into several sections grouping related rules. Each rule is associated with a unique identifier and a textual description. The titles of the files and sections provide contextual information about the business concepts or functional areas addressed by the rules. Explicit links between rules and their implementations are available only in a small number of cases; these cases are used to construct the ground truth for the evaluation of our approach.

This combination of a large, evolving codebase and structured business-rule documentation reflects conditions commonly found in industrial software systems. It therefore provides a suitable context for investigating how textual descriptions of business rules relate to their potential implementations in source code.
