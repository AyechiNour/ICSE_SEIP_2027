\begin{abstract}

Business rules govern business processes in enterprise applications, yet their implementations are often scattered across large codebases and intertwined with technical details, making them difficult to locate during software maintenance and evolution. Determining which methods implement a business rule based on its textual description is therefore a challenging task in large industrial systems. 
In this paper, we address this challenge in the context of a real-world industrial codebase. We propose a fully automated approach that combines source code analysis, semantic similarity, and information extracted from business-rule documentation to identify candidate methods that are likely to implement a given business rule. We evaluate our approach on a set of business rules from the industrial system. The results show that our approach substantially reduces the number of candidate methods while retaining a high proportion of the methods implementing the rules. These findings highlight the potential of our approach to support developers in locating business-rule implementations and facilitate software maintenance and evolution.

\end{abstract}
