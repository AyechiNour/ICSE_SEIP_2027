\begin{abstract}

Business rules play a central role in enterprise applications, as they capture organizational policies, legal requirements, and decision logic that govern business processes. In legacy systems, however, these rules are often embedded in large codebases, scattered across different parts of the system, and mixed with technical implementation details. As a result, when a business rule changes, developers must identify the parts of the source code that implement the rule before making the necessary modifications. This task can be time-consuming and error-prone, particularly in large and complex codebases.

Locating the source code that implements business rules has attracted attention from both academic research and industrial practice. In this paper, we address this industrial problem in the context of a real-world industrial codebase. To this end, we propose a fully automated approach to assist developers in locating the code that may need to be modified when a business rule changes. Given a textual description of a business rule, our approach uses embeddings to identify potential relationships between the rule and the source code. It then combines this semantic analysis with source code analysis to progressively filter the methods and retain those that are most relevant to the rule. The approach aims to reduce the search space by identifying candidate methods that are likely to implement the affected business rule. We evaluate our approach on a real-world industrial codebase and show that it can substantially narrow down the set of candidate methods while retaining a high proportion of the methods that implement the rule. These results suggest that the approach can provide useful support for developers during software maintenance and evolution tasks.

\end{abstract}
